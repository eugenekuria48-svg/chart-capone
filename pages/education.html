Market Structure

Swing highs/lows, trends, BOS, MSS and CHoCH.

Beginner~2.5h10 lessons

Understanding Market Structure

Market structure is the way price organizes its highs and lows over time.

Understanding structure can help traders determine whether a market is showing bullish, bearish, or changing conditions.

Basic Structure

A bullish market may create:

Higher High → Higher Low → Higher High

A bearish market may create:

Lower Low → Lower High → Lower Low

These relationships help traders understand the current price environment.

BOS — Break of Structure

A Break of Structure (BOS) occurs when price breaks an important structural high or low.

Traders often use BOS to help identify continuation in the current direction.

MSS / CHoCH

A Market Structure Shift (MSS) or Change of Character (CHoCH) is commonly used to describe a potential change in the behavior of price.

However: One structure break doesn't automatically mean the entire trend has reversed. Context matters.

Chart Capone Perspective

We study structure alongside liquidity, supply & demand, and price reaction.

The objective isn't to predict every candle. It's to understand what price is doing before deciding what to do.

Liquidity

Stop hunts, equal highs/lows, liquidity runs and reactions.

Intermediate~2h9 lessons

Understanding Liquidity in Forex

Liquidity is one of the most important concepts in modern price-action trading.

In simple terms, liquidity refers to areas where a large amount of buying or selling interest may exist. Traders often watch areas around previous highs and lows, equal highs and lows, and other obvious price levels.

Where Can Liquidity Be Found?

Common areas include:

  • Previous highs
  • Previous lows
  • Equal highs
  • Equal lows
  • Session highs and lows
  • Obvious swing points

For example, when several traders place stop-loss orders around a similar high, that area may become an important liquidity zone.

Liquidity Sweeps

Price doesn't always move directly toward its eventual direction. Sometimes price briefly moves beyond an obvious high or low before reversing.

This is commonly referred to as a liquidity sweep or liquidity grab.

The important lesson:

A liquidity sweep by itself is not automatically a trading signal. The surrounding market structure and price reaction still matter.

Chart Capone Perspective

At Chart Capone, liquidity is studied together with market structure and supply & demand rather than treated as an isolated entry signal.

Supply & Demand

Identifying zones, freshness, retests and institutional footprints.

Intermediate~2h8 lessons

Understanding Supply & Demand Zones

Supply and demand are fundamental concepts used to understand areas where buying and selling pressure may become significant.

Demand Zones

A demand zone represents an area where buying interest may have previously entered the market. When price returns to the area, traders may watch for signs of another reaction.

Supply Zones

A supply zone represents an area where selling interest may have previously influenced price. When price returns, traders may watch how price behaves around the zone.

What Makes a Zone Interesting?

Traders may consider:

  • The strength of the move away
  • How fresh the zone is
  • Whether price has previously revisited it
  • The surrounding market structure
  • Liquidity nearby
  • The timeframe of the zone

But simply drawing a rectangle on a chart isn't enough. Context matters.

Chart Capone Perspective

We don't view supply and demand zones in isolation. They become more interesting when they interact with liquidity and market structure.

SMC Concepts

Order blocks, fair value gaps, imbalances and smart money flow.

Intermediate~3h12 lessons

Introduction to Smart Money Concepts

Smart Money Concepts (SMC) is a collection of price-action ideas used by traders to study market structure, liquidity, imbalances, and areas where significant price movement may have occurred.

Commonly Discussed SMC Concepts

  • Break of Structure
  • Market Structure Shift
  • Liquidity
  • Order Blocks
  • Fair Value Gaps
  • Imbalances
  • Premium & Discount
  • Supply & Demand

Fair Value Gaps

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is commonly described as an imbalance created by rapid price movement. Traders may monitor these areas because price can sometimes revisit them.

Order Blocks

An Order Block is a price area that traders identify as potentially significant before a strong price movement.

However, not every candle that looks like an order block automatically creates a high-quality setup.

The Bigger Picture

SMC concepts become more useful when they're studied as part of a larger framework.

At Chart Capone, we focus on the relationship between:

Structure + Liquidity + Supply & Demand

rather than treating individual concepts as automatic buy/sell signals.

Risk Management

Position sizing, risk %, R:R, drawdown control and capital protection.

All levels~1.5h7 lessons

Protecting Your Trading Capital

Risk management is one of the most important parts of trading.

A trader can have a strong strategy and still fail if risk is not controlled.

Before thinking about how much you can make, you should understand how much you're willing to lose.

Important Risk Concepts

Position Size

The size of a trade should be connected to the amount of risk being taken.

Stop Loss

A stop-loss defines a point where the trading idea is considered invalid.

Risk-to-Reward

Risk-to-reward compares the potential loss of a trade with its potential profit.

For example, a setup risking $10 to potentially make $20 has a 1:2 risk-to-reward relationship.

Why Risk Management Matters

No strategy wins every trade. Losses are part of trading.

Good risk management means that a single losing trade shouldn't destroy your ability to continue trading.

Chart Capone Principle

Protect the account first. Look for opportunities second.
